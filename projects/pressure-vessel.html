Pressure Vessel Design / ASME VIII-1 & FEA

150 psig Compressed-Air Receiver

Design Basis

Code: ASME BPVC Section VIII, Div. 1 (2017)

Design: 150 psig at 200°F, MDMT −20°F

Vessel MAWP: 175 psig

Materials

Shell and heads: SA-516 Gr. 70 plate

Nozzles: SA-106 Gr. B pipe, NPS 6 Sch 80

Tools

Hand calculations (Python-verified), SolidWorks, ANSYS Mechanical

Executive Summary

Horizontal Compressed-Air Receiver, Design to ASME VIII-1
Designed a 48 in ID horizontal air receiver for 150 psig at 200°F, with a 3/8 in shell, 7/16 in torispherical heads and an NPS 6 Sch 80 nozzle on the centerline of each head. Worked the shell, head, MAWP, hydrotest, nozzle neck, opening reinforcement and attachment weld calculations by hand, rebuilt them to match the SolidWorks model as it evolved, and checked the finished model in ANSYS Mechanical. The FEA agrees with the hand calculations to within about 1.5% on shell and head membrane stress.

Design Approach

  • Design basis first. Pressure, temperature, material, joint efficiency and corrosion allowance were fixed up front, and every input is tagged as an assumption, a code value or a model dimension.
  • Hand calculations track the model. Each time the SolidWorks model changed (head type, nozzle size and position, pad, saddle radius), the calculations were rerun against the exported geometry. Head thickness went from a 2:1 ellipsoidal estimate to the 7/16 in torispherical head that is actually modeled.
  • FEA as an independent check. The full model was loaded with 150 psig internal pressure plus the nozzle end thrust and compared against the hand values on linearized stress paths, not on peak stress.

2. Impact Test Exemption, UG-20(f)

All five conditions are met: P-No. 1 Gr. 1 or 2 material at most 1 in thick (shell 0.375 in, head 0.4375 in, neck 0.432 in), hydrotest per UG-99(b), design temperature between −20°F and 650°F, and no thermal shock or cyclic loading (assumed). Impact testing per UG-84 is not required.

3. Shell Thickness, UG-27(c)

tcirc = PR / (SE − 0.6P) = 150(24.125) / (20,000(0.85) − 0.6(150)) = 0.2140 in
tlong = PR / (2SE + 0.4P) = 150(24.125) / (2(20,000)(0.85) + 0.4(150)) = 0.1062 in

The circumferential case governs. With the corrosion allowance the requirement is 0.339 in, so the 3/8 in shell passes. Shell MAWP, using the corroded thickness of 0.250 in:

Pshell = SEt / (R + 0.6t) = 20,000(0.85)(0.250) / (24.125 + 0.6(0.250)) = 175.1 psig

4. Torispherical Head, UG-32(e)

Corroded radii: L = 37.633 + 0.125 = 37.758 in and r = 3.952 + 0.125 = 4.077 in.

M = ¼ (3 + √(L/r)) = ¼ (3 + √9.261) = 1.511
t = PLM / (2SE − 0.2P) = 150(37.758)(1.511) / (2(20,000)(0.85) − 0.2(150)) = 0.2519 in

With the corrosion allowance this is 0.3769 in. At 175 psig it is 0.2939 + 0.125 = 0.4189 in. The model's 0.4375 in head covers both. Head MAWP with t = 0.4375 − 0.125 = 0.3125 in:

Phead = 2SEt / (LM + 0.2t) = 2(20,000)(0.85)(0.3125) / (37.758(1.511) + 0.2(0.3125)) = 186.1 psig

Geometry limits: knuckle radius is 8.1% of the skirt outside diameter (at least 6%) and at least 3t, and t/L = 0.0116 (at least 0.002). Inside head depth excluding the flange: 10.57 in. A 3/8 in head would only reach 148.9 psig, which is why the heads were increased to 7/16 in.

5. MAWP and Hydrostatic Test, UG-98 and UG-99

The shell (175.1 psig) is the weakest part, ahead of the head (186.1 psig), so the vessel MAWP is 175 psig.

Ptest = 1.3 × MAWP × (Stest / Sdesign) = 1.3 × 175 × 1.0 = 227.5 psig

6. Nozzle Neck Thickness, UG-45

tb1 uses the required head thickness at the nozzle with E = 1 (crown-radius sphere), plus c:

tb1 = PL / (2S − 0.2P) + c = 150(37.758) / (2(20,000) − 0.2(150)) + 0.125 = 0.2667 in
tb3 = 0.245 + 0.125 = 0.370 in (Table UG-45, NPS 6), so tb = 0.2667 in
ta = PRn / (SnE − 0.6P) + c = 150(3.006) / (17,100 − 90) + 0.125 = 0.1515 in

Required: 0.2667 in. Sch 80 minimum wall 0.432 × 0.875 = 0.378 in passes. Sch 40 (0.245 in) fails. Pipe wall and undertolerance values are to be checked against B36.10M and SA-106.

8. Attachment Weld Strength, UG-41 and UW-16

The model shows no welds, so this check assumes the usual detail for a set-on nozzle: a full-penetration groove weld to the head, a 1/4 in fillet from the nozzle to the pad, and a 1/4 in fillet around the pad edge. UW-15(c) stresses: groove weld in tension 74%, fillet shear 49%, nozzle wall shear 70%. Treat this check as simplified. Load to carry at 175 psig:

9. FEA Validation (ANSYS Mechanical)

Setup

  • Static structural, linear elastic, full 29-body model imported from the SolidWorks STEP file, with bonded contacts between bodies and a single Structural Steel material (E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.3).
  • Internal pressure of 1.0342 MPa (150 psi) on all inside faces, plus an outward end force of 17,393 N (3,910 lbf) on each nozzle end to represent the pressure thrust of the open nozzle.
  • Saddle supports with no gravity, so the reactions should sum to near zero. Net residual: about 0.2% of the total pressure thrust.
  • Stresses read on linearized paths through the wall: shell mid-span, head crown and head near the nozzle opening. Peak stress contours are not used, because bonded sharp corners are singularities.

  • Pad bore. The pad's hole is the nozzle ID, so the nozzle sits on top of the pad. A real pad has a hole the size of the nozzle OD. The calculations treat the pad as a ring from the nozzle OD outward.
  • No manhole. A 48 in compressed-air vessel needs an inspection opening under UG-46 (exemptions to be verified, including whether the two NPS 6 nozzles could serve). As modeled, the vessel is not code-complete for that requirement.
  • Saddles. Centered 22.5 in inside each tangent line (75 in apart) and sized by eye. They are not designed (no Zick analysis), so stresses near the saddles are not design values.
  • Not modeled: welds, pad vent holes (UG-37(g)), flanges and anchor bolts.
  • Div. 2 comparison. The FEA stresses are far below any plausible Div. 2 allowable, but the Table 5A allowable stress has not been looked up yet, so no formal Pm / PL / Pb limit check is shown. Div. 2 is also a different code from the Div. 1 hand calculations, and the FEA uses nominal thickness where the code uses corroded thickness.
  • To verify: B36.10M wall thicknesses and the SA-106 undertolerance; the UG-32(e) formula and limits; the UG-37(a) wording for torispherical heads; the UG-41 paths for a set-on nozzle; the UG-46 requirements. Allowable stresses (II-D 2019) and rules (Div. 1 2017) are from different editions.
  • Not covered: saddle design, flange rating and external loads.
